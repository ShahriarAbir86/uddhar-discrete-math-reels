# Uddhar — Discrete Mathematics Explainer Series Bible

## Purpose
Make university-level Discrete Mathematics concepts understandable to CSE students through short, accurate, visually memorable explanations. The video should reduce conceptual friction, not merely decorate a lecture.

## Audience
University CSE students, especially learners encountering a concept for the first time or revising it for coursework/exams. Do not assume one university's syllabus unless explicitly supplied.

## Editorial promise
- Explain one central learning objective per episode.
- Start with a familiar question, object, puzzle or misconception.
- Reveal the mathematical idea visually before naming it.
- Move from intuition to formal notation to a concrete worked example.
- Connect to Computer Science only when the connection is accurate and useful.
- End with a compact conceptual takeaway.

## Tone
Friendly, curious, clear, academically credible, conversational Bangla with natural English technical terms. Avoid stiff textbook narration, forced slang, condescension, exam-result guarantees, or fear-based messaging.

## Typical episode flow (adapt to narration)
1. Hook / question.
2. Familiar visual metaphor or problem.
3. Name the concept.
4. Explain the core rule/definition.
5. Work through one example.
6. Show a relevant CS connection.
7. Payoff / memory anchor.

## Length
Usually 60–120 seconds depending on the approved voiceover. The actual recording controls runtime. Do not compress or speed up the voice merely to hit an arbitrary target.

## Accuracy protocol
- Verify definitions, formulas, notation, assumptions, worked steps and CS claims.
- Use reputable sources: standard textbooks, university course notes, official course materials, peer-reviewed sources where appropriate.
- Keep a `references.md` file with title, institution/author, URL, and the exact claim supported.
- Distinguish intuition/metaphor from formal proof.
- If a claim is uncertain, flag it rather than inventing an explanation.
- Do not imply that a single example proves a universal statement.

## Visual storytelling
One main diagram/world per episode. It may have connected zones or branches, but the viewer should understand that they belong to one continuous composition. The camera may pan/zoom through that composition; objects should not teleport or rearrange merely to fit a new scene.

## Text density
- Aim for <=7 explanatory words visible simultaneously.
- Mathematical expressions may exceed this limit when they are the actual lesson; break derivations into readable steps.
- Labels attach to the relevant object/equation.
- No detached legend if labels can be placed beside the objects.
- Subtitles are separate from diagram labels and must follow the audio/subtitle rules.
